## Part A — Why did the policy miss 30 of the 69?

The audit reported: *"The first loader run exposed 30 re-marked restored codes."*

`deriveConfirmedDiscontinuations()` now implements all three parts of the withdrawal rule, and its unit test passes. So the pure function is right. But on a real load it produced the wrong answer for **30 of the 69 restored codes** — which means the **caller** is not populating `markerPeriod` or `latestPricedPeriod` correctly for every sheet.

The manifest reassertion currently in place forces the 69/90 split back after each load. That works today and hides the defect. It also freezes those 159 codes permanently: if Prayag genuinely withdraws one of the 69, or reintroduces one of the 90, or a future workbook moves a marker, the manifest overrides the source and nothing surfaces it.

### Report

1. **Which 30 codes** were re-marked on that first run. List them with division and the sheet each came from.
2. **Group them.** Are they one division, one sheet, one workbook? The 69 split into 61 shared-component cases (CP `MASTER`, keyed by range+code) and 8 water tanks (marker in the 01 May 2024 column). Do the 30 fall into one of those groups, or cut across both?
3. **For three representative codes**, dump the exact `SourceRemovalRow[]` the caller built — every row, with `markerPeriod`, `latestPricedPeriod` and `isRemoved` as populated. Then state which field was wrong and what it should have been.
4. **Name the mechanism.** Likely candidates, in rough order: `markerPeriod` left null because the sheet's marker column has no date header; `latestPricedPeriod` computed per-row instead of per-code; a sheet whose layout the caller reads through a different path than CP `MASTER`. Say which it is, or that it is none of these.

Include `WT-3LH-50` in the three if it was among the 30 — its shape is fully understood from the source, so it is the cleanest specimen.

**Do not remove the manifest reassertion.** It is the only thing holding the correct state right now. The question is whether it is a stopgap over a small caller bug or a permanent crutch, and that depends on what the 30 turn out to be.

Two rows could be new periods for products already live, and two products could have become live for another reason — a cleared `discontinued_from`, an `is_active` flip.
