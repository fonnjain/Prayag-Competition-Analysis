Product `151` (Ball Cock with Rod & Ball 15mm) currently shows:

```

Sparsh   ₹410.00   valid 5 Mar – 31 Aug 2026   decreases to ₹330.00 on 1 Sept (−19.5%)
```

Every figure needed is on the screen. The one that matters is not:

```
From 1 Sept 2026:  ₹336.00 vs ₹330.00  →  −1.8%
```

**Today Prayag is 28.8% cheaper. On 1 September Prayag becomes 1.8% more expensive.** A 31-point swing and a reversal of position — and the page shows neither. A reader must notice two separate "changes soon" notes and do the arithmetic.

## What to build

### The upcoming gap

Wherever a current gap is shown, also show the gap at the next date on which **either** side changes, when such a date exists.

Compute it exactly as the current gap is computed, but resolve **both** prices at that future date. Never the future price of one side against the current price of the other — that mismatch is what produced the false +10.8% figure previously.

### Handle the general case

The two sides will not always change on the same day. The next change date is **the earliest future effective date across both sides**; resolve each side at that date, which may leave one side's price unchanged.

If several future dates exist, use the nearest. One is enough for a product page — do not render a chain.

If neither side has a future price, show nothing. No empty slot, no repetition of the current gap.

### State the direction in words

A negative percentage beside the word "gap" is ambiguous — the reader cannot tell whether Prayag is cheaper or dearer without knowing the convention:

```
From 1 Sept 2026:  −1.8%  ·  Prayag becomes more expensive
```

### Mark the reversals

A gap changing sign is a different event from a gap merely narrowing, and should read differently. A product where Prayag is cheaper today and dearer from 1 September is the single most useful thing this page can tell a salesperson.

Reuse the existing "changes soon" visual language rather than inventing new treatment — it is already established on both cards.
