# Replit Prompt — Show the Upcoming Gap, Not Just the Upcoming Prices

The validity windows now work on both sides. What is missing is the comparison between them at the future date.

## What the product page shows today

For product `151` (Ball Cock with Rod & Ball 15mm):

```
Prayag   ₹318.24   valid 5 Mar – 31 Aug 2026   increases to ₹336.00 on 1 Sept (+5.6%)
Sparsh   ₹410.00   valid 5 Mar – 31 Aug 2026   decreases to ₹330.00 on 1 Sept (−19.5%)
Current gap  +28.8%
```

Every number needed is on the screen. The one that matters is not:

```
From 1 Sept 2026:  ₹336.00 vs ₹330.00  →  −1.8%
```

**Today Prayag is 28.8% cheaper. On 1 September Prayag becomes 1.8% more expensive.** A 31-point swing and a reversal of position, and the page shows neither — a reader has to notice two separate "changes soon" notes and do the arithmetic themselves.

## What to build

### The upcoming gap

Wherever a current gap is shown, show the gap at the next date on which **either** side changes, when such a date exists.

Compute it the same way the current gap is computed, but resolve both prices at that future date: the Prayag price in force then, against the competitor price in force then. **Both sides resolved at the same date** — never the future price of one against the current price of the other.

For product 151 that is 1 Sept 2026: ₹336.00 against ₹330.00, giving −1.8%.

### Handle the general case, not just this one

The two sides will not always change on the same day. Determine the next change date as **the earliest future effective date across both sides**, then resolve each side at that date — which may mean one side's price is unchanged.

If more than one future change date exists, use the nearest. Do not show a chain of future gaps; one is enough for a product page.

If neither side has a future price, show nothing. Do not render an empty slot or repeat the current gap.

### State the direction in words

A negative percentage next to the word "gap" is ambiguous — a reader cannot tell whether it means Prayag is cheaper or dearer without knowing the convention. Say it:

```
From 1 Sept 2026:  −1.8%  ·  Prayag becomes more expensive
```

### Mark the reversals

When the gap changes sign, that is a different event from the gap merely narrowing, and it should read differently. A product where Prayag is cheaper today and dearer from 1 September is the single most useful thing this page can tell a salesperson.

Use the existing "changes soon" visual language rather than inventing a new one — it is already established on both cards.

---

## Where else this belongs

The product page is the first place, but the same figure matters on the list views. On the Comparison and Competition Analysis pages, add the ability to see the gap **as of a chosen date**, so someone can view the whole range as it will stand on 1 September rather than reading products one at a time.

If a date selector already exists there, ensure it drives both sides consistently. If not, treat this as a separate follow-up rather than expanding this task — the product page is the priority.
