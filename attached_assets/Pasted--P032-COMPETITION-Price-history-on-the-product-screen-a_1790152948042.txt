P031 established that the history is complete — 14,102 rows across 16 periods — and that the public product screen deliberately shows only the current price and the next one. Two `LIMIT 1` queries, no history.

That is a reasonable default and it stays. What is missing is a way to see the rest **on the screen people actually use**.

Two additions, and they are the same idea from opposite sides of today:

```
Part 1   what the price has been      <- looking back
Part 2   what the gap becomes next    <- looking forward
```

# PART 1 — Price history on the product screen

## 1.1 Reuse the endpoint that already works

The Analysis price-history endpoint already returns every approved Standard MRP row for a product, ordered by effective date and id, with no date floor, no `LIMIT` and no `is_current` filter:

```
artifacts/api-server/src/routes/analysis.ts:836-843
```

**Reuse it. Do not write a second query.** Two queries answering the same question drift apart, and the one nobody is watching drifts first. If it needs a public-facing wrapper for auth or shaping, wrap it — do not reimplement it.

Report what you did: reused directly, wrapped, or duplicated and why.

## 1.2 What it looks like

A **Price history** section on the product detail, **collapsed by default**. The screen's job today is to answer "what is the price" in one glance, and that must not change.

Expanded, one row per period, newest first:

```
Effective from    MRP          Change        Source
01 Sep 2026       Rs 36.00     +Rs 2.00  +5.9%   authoritative master v2
01 Aug 2026       Rs 36.00           —          authoritative master v2
01 May 2026       Rs 36.00     +Rs 2.00  +5.9%   all-periods rebuild
05 Mar 2026       Rs 34.00           —          all-periods rebuild
...
```

- **Change** is against the previous row for that product. The oldest row has no change — render a dash, never `0%` and never `+100%`.
- **Source** comes from the load batch. If a row has no `load_batch_id`, say `unknown` rather than guessing. `nullLoadBatchCount` was 0 at last check, so this should never appear — if it does, report it.
- Rows are never recomputed on the fly. Display what is stored.

## 1.3 Two rows is normal, not an error

**81% of products have exactly two price rows** — 5,117 of 6,303 in the source. A product showing two periods is correct and complete.

Do not add a warning, an empty-state message, or a "limited history" notice for short histories. Do not pad. A product with one row shows one row.

Three products have nine periods (`TTS-01`, `TTS-02`, `TTS-03`); 246 have five. Those are the exceptions.

## 1.4 Indian digit grouping

Every figure in this section groups the Indian way — thousand, lakh, crore. `Rs 1,00,000.00`, not `Rs 100,000.00`.

**P024 Job B has not been delivered yet.** If the shared `Intl.NumberFormat("en-IN")` formatter does not exist, build this section against it anyway and say in your report that the formatter is still outstanding. Do not write a local one-off formatter that P024 will later have to unpick.

## 1.5 Who can see it

State plainly in your report **who reaches this screen** — signed-in users only, or anyone with the URL.

If the Price Finder is public, full MRP history is a commercial disclosure and the section must be behind a sign-in. **If you are not certain, put it behind a sign-in and say so.** That is a reversible decision; publishing three years of pricing is not.

# PART 2 — The gap after the next change

## 2.1 What P004 asked for, and what has changed since

P004 described product `151` showing a current gap of +3.8% and becoming −1.8% on 01 September. **That date is now in the past.** The September revision took effect on 01 September and those 31 reversals have already happened — they are history now, not forecast. They are visible only through Part 1.

So build the feature, but **do not use P004's examples as acceptance tests.** They will fail, correctly.

**Check this before building:** the latest effective date in `mrp_price_history` is `2026-09-01`. Today is later than that. **There may be no future-dated price anywhere in the database**, which means this feature will render nothing on every product.

Report the count of future-dated rows first. If it is zero, that is expected — the feature is for the next revision, and you must test it against a seeded future date in development rather than concluding it does not work.

## 2.2 The rule

Wherever a current gap appears, also show the gap at the **next date on which either side changes**, when such a date exists.

Compute it exactly as the current gap is computed, but resolve **both** prices at that future date.

**Never one side's future price against the other's current price.** That mismatch produced a false +10.8% reading earlier in this project and took a week to unwind. It is the single most important line in this prompt.

The next change date is the **earliest future effective date across both sides**. Resolve each side at that date — often one side is unchanged, which is correct. If several future dates exist, use the nearest; one is enough for a product page.

If neither side has a future price, **render nothing**. No empty slot, no placeholder, no repetition of the current gap.

## 2.3 State the direction in words

`−1.8%` beside the word "gap" is ambiguous without knowing the convention:

```
From 01 Dec 2026:   −1.8%   ·   Prayag becomes more expensive
```

## 2.4 Mark reversals distinctly

A gap changing **sign** is a different event from a gap narrowing. `+7.7%` → `−0.3%` is a reversal. `+20%` → `+12%` is not.

Reuse the existing "changes soon" visual language rather than inventing new treatment.

## 2.5 Where else it belongs

The **Comparison** page. A chosen set of products is exactly where "what happens at the next revision" gets asked. Add the upcoming gap there too, or make the page filterable by an as-of date. If a date selector already exists, ensure it drives **both** sides consistently.
