# Replit Prompt 110 — How MRP reaches this app, and why 870 codes have none

**Date:** 18 September 2026
**Scope:** All read-only. Change nothing.

**Standing rule:** every figure states which database or source it came from.

---

## Two questions, and the second explains the first

**Q7:** 870 sold codes have no MRP, worth ₹11.03 Cr — 328 selling steadily at
₹5.19 Cr. That should not be possible for a product being sold.

**The cadence question:** how often does this app fetch MRP from the pricing app,
and what happens to a code the pricing app does not yet know about?

**And one interpretation to apply throughout:** a code with **no historic price
for a period is treated as not offered in that period**, not as a price of zero
and not as missing data. A product that had no price in March was not for sale in
March.

## Section C — historic prices and the "not offered" rule

```
Read-only. This establishes an interpretation to apply consistently.

RULE TO APPLY: a code with no price effective in a given period was NOT
OFFERED in that period. It is not a zero price and not missing data.

C1. Does mrp_history support period-effective lookup - "what was this
    code's price in March 2026" - or does it only hold current prices with
    an effective date?

    The MRP cache holds 6,109 rows from the pricing app. mrp_history was
    found stale and only affecting price-shrinker detection.

C2. FOR EACH OF THE 870, was there EVER a price? Print:
      never priced in any period        -> new or never offered
      priced previously, not now        -> withdrawn
      priced now, not when it sold      -> the interesting case

    The third group is the one that matters: a product that sold in a month
    where it had no effective price.

C3. HOW DOES THE APP CURRENTLY TREAT a sale in a period with no effective
    price? Does it use the nearest price, the current price, or exclude the
    row?

    Using the CURRENT price against a past sale would compute a discount
    against a price that did not exist at the time. Print which it does.

C4. Under the not-offered rule, those rows should be EXCLUDED from discount
    and realisation calculations, with the exclusion counted and stated -
    not silently priced at the nearest available figure.

    Print how much value would be excluded on that basis, per FY.
```

## Notes

- **A2 is the question I would read first.** If the refresh is still
  restart-driven, the price catalogue is as fresh as the last deployment rather
  than the last price change — and that alone could explain codes the pricing app
  has and we do not.
- **B1 splits the problem in one query.** Present in the pricing app but absent
  here is our defect; absent in both is Prayag's. The 870 are currently being
  treated as one thing and they are two.
- **C3 is the quiet risk.** Applying today's price to a past sale computes a
  discount against a price that did not exist then. The not-offered rule is the
  correct treatment and it needs to be stated rather than assumed.
- **B4 matters for the number itself.** Roughly ₹2.9 Cr of the ₹11.03 Cr is
  already explained. Quoting 870 unexplained codes overstates the gap, and the
  residue is the figure worth chasing.
