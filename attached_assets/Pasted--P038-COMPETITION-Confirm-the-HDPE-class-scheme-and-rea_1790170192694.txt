# PART 1 — The HDPE class scheme

```

 54   3 new classes (P, X, R)  x  18 existing sizes 20-315 mm
 25   the 3 new classes at the new sizes

```

The class suffix encodes grade and pressure class. Read off the master's own product names:

```

J L M N O      PE-80     SDR 26 / 17 / 13.6 / 11 / 9
Q S T U V W    PE-100    SDR 33 / 21 / 17 / 13.6 / 11 / 9
```

`P`, `X` and `R` appear nowhere in the master.

```
R   PE-100 SDR 26     0.640 MPa    probably labelled PN 5
```

**Confirm or correct it from the stored names. Do not assume I am right.**

2. **The `product_name`, `size`, `mrp` and `effective_date` of all 33 production PE-63 codes** — the `D`, `F` and `G` suffixes at sizes 355 mm to 1200 mm.

These matter because of §1.2.

## 1.2 Why the 33 matter — do not act on this, just report

The 155-product name change corrects PE-63 naming:

```

SDR-17 -> SDR-13.6    PN-6 unchanged
SDR-13.6 -> SDR-11    PN-8 unchanged
```

That correction is right. Measured against the catalogue's own convention — read off PE-80 and PE-100 independently, where PN 4 = 0.50-0.51 MPa, PN 6 = 0.80, PN 8 = 1.00-1.02 — the **old** PE-63 names fall short of their stated class by exactly **21.3%**, which is precisely the PE-63 to PE-80 material ratio. The old rows are the PE-80 rows with the grade swapped and the wall never re-derated.

**The 33 production PE-63 codes carry that same old naming and are not in the name-change batch.**

If the name change runs as it stands, the catalogue holds 50 PE-63 codes correctly named at 20-315 mm and 33 wrongly named at 355-1200 mm. One family, two contradictory schemes, split by diameter.
