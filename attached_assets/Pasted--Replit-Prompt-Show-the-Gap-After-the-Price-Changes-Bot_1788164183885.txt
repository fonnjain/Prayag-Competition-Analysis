# Replit Prompt — Show the Gap After the Price Changes

Both sides of the product page now carry validity windows and a "changes soon" note. What is missing is the comparison **after** the change.

---

## The gap in the display

Product `151`, Ball Cock with Rod & Ball 15mm, as it renders today:

```
Prayag   ₹318.00   valid 05-03-2026 – 31-08-2026   increases to ₹336.00 on 01-09-2026 (+5.7%)
Sparsh   ₹330.00   valid from 01-07-2026
Current gap  +3.8%
```

Everything needed is on the screen. The one figure that matters is not:

```
From 01 Sep 2026:  ₹336.00 vs ₹330.00  →  −1.8%  ·  Prayag becomes more expensive
```

Today Prayag is 3.8% cheaper. On 01 September it is 1.8% dearer. The reader has to spot a "changes soon" note on one card, notice the other card has none, and do the arithmetic.

---

## Why this matters more than it did

Sparsh raised prices on **01 July**. Prayag raises on **01 September**. So the two sides move at different times, and the whole mapped set shifts one way:

| | Today | From 01 Sep |
|---|---:|---:|
| Median gap | **+10.8%** | **+2.9%** |
| Prayag cheaper on | 130 / 150 | 98 / 150 |

**31 products change competitive position on 01 September — all 31 against Prayag. None in Prayag's favour.**

That asymmetry is the point. This is not a mixed picture where some items improve; it is 31 products crossing from cheaper to dearer on a single day, and nothing on screen shows it.

A sample:

| Code | Product | Today | 01 Sep |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| `124-V` | Bib Cock Long Body 15mm | +0.6% | **−7.2%** |
| `168` | Ball Cock Rod (Aluminium) 20mm | +1.0% | **−6.2%** |
| `127` | Bib Cock with Nozzle 15mm | +3.6% | **−4.7%** |
| `144-V` | Angle Cock with Flange | +3.4% | **−4.7%** |
| `124` | Bib Cock Long Body 15mm | +3.1% | **−4.7%** |
| `343` | Corrugated Pipe (Inlet) Extra Long | +3.5% | **−4.3%** |
| `151` | Ball Cock with Rod & Ball 15mm | +3.8% | **−1.8%** |

Several sit near zero today — `124-V` at +0.6%, `168` at +1.0%, `121` at +4.1%. Nobody scanning the current screen would pick those out as the ones about to invert.

These figures are computed from the source. **Report your own from the database**; if they differ, that difference is worth knowing.

---

## What to build

### The upcoming gap

Wherever a current gap appears, also show the gap at the next date on which **either** side changes, when such a date exists.

Compute it exactly as the current gap is computed, but resolve **both** prices at that future date. Never one side's future price against the other's current price — that mismatch produced a false +10.8% reading earlier in this project and took a week to unwind.

### Handle the general case

The two sides will not always change on the same day. For `151`, only Prayag changes on 01 September; Sparsh has been on Vol 6.2 since 01 July and is unchanged.

The next change date is **the earliest future effective date across both sides**. Resolve each side at that date — which will often mean one side's price is unchanged. If several future dates exist, use the nearest; one is enough for a product page.

If neither side has a future price, render nothing. No empty slot, no repetition of the current gap.

### State the direction in words

`−1.8%` beside the word "gap" is ambiguous without knowing the convention:

```
From 01 Sep 2026:  −1.8%  ·  Prayag becomes more expensive
```

### Mark the reversals distinctly

A gap changing sign is a different event from a gap narrowing. `1375-S` moving +7.7% → −0.3% and `150-C` moving +7.8% → −0.2% are reversals; a product going +20% → +12% is not. Reuse the existing "changes soon" visual language rather than inventing new treatment.

---

## Where else this belongs

The **Comparison** page — now that products can be selected from Price Finder, a chosen set is exactly where "what happens on 01 September" gets asked. Add the upcoming gap there too, or make the page filterable by an as-of date.

If a date selector already exists, ensure it drives both sides consistently.

## Acceptance tests

```
1.  Product 151 shows an upcoming gap of −1.8% at 01 Sep 2026, worded to say
    Prayag becomes more expensive.
2.  Product 124-V shows −7.2%, marked as a reversal from +0.6%.
3.  A product whose gap narrows but keeps its sign is NOT marked a reversal.
4.  A product where only one side has a future price resolves correctly —
    both sides at the same date, one of them unchanged.
5.  A product with no future price on either side shows no upcoming element.
6.  Across the 150, the count changing competitive position on 01 Sep is
    reported. Expected around 31, all unfavourable.
7.  Current gaps are unchanged everywhere. This adds a figure; it must not
    alter the existing one.
```

Test 7 is the guard. Current gaps have been verified against source repeatedly; nothing here should move them.
