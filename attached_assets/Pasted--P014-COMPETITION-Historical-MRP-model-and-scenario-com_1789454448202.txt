# P014 — COMPETITION — Historical MRP model and scenario comparison

> **P014** · app: **COMPETITION** (`prayag-price.com`)
> **THIS PROMPT CHANGES CODE.**
> Quote `P014` when reporting back.

Two additions. **Compute MRP (Historical)** — a second, independent way to suggest prices, built from how Prayag has actually revised MRP rather than from what competitors charge. And a **Comparison tab** for putting scenarios side by side.

---

## 1. The pattern is real, and it is at series level

Measured across the 01 Sep 2026 revision, 5,580 products with a prior price:

```
series with 5+ products                      110
of which apply a NEAR-UNIFORM increase        92
```

Examples — p10 to p90 spread inside a single series:

```
1200 Series 304 Super Steel    n=41    7.99%    7.95% - 8.09%
Garden Pipe                    n=45    8.04%    7.89% - 8.09%
Helix                          n=31    7.94%    7.65% - 8.20%
Diamond                        n=29    8.04%    7.63% - 8.47%
304 Super Steel (0.85 Mm)      n=83    5.95%    5.36% - 6.25%
```

**Prayag sets a target percentage per series per revision.** That is a learnable rule, and 92 of 110 series follow it closely.

The 18 that do not are mostly Hardware — `SET` at 41.4% spanning 6.6% to 59.7%, `NOS` at 39.0% spanning 11.7% to 97.8%. Those were resets, not routine revisions, and the model must say so rather than averaging them.

**By period, the house rate is visible too:**

```
01 Sep 2026   median  8.02%      01 Aug 2026   median  9.31%
10 Aug 2026   median 14.97%      01 May 2026   median  4.67%
01 Feb 2026   median  0.00%   <- most products unchanged
```

### The constraint that shapes the design

**The median product has only two price points.** A per-product trend line fitted to two observations is not a trend, it is a straight line through two dots.

So the model works **at series level** — where there are 29 to 124 products moving together — and applies the series rate to the individual product. Never fit a curve to a single product's history.

## 2. Compute MRP (Historical)

A button beside **New Scenario**. It produces a run like any other, with its own analysis.

### What it computes

For each series:

```
revision cadence     months between revisions, and how many observed
typical rate         median increase per revision for this series
consistency          p10-p90 spread; is the series uniform or not
months since last    for each product
due                  cadence - months since last
```

Then per product:

```
suggested = current × (1 + series_rate)
```

Applied only where the series is **uniform** and the product is **due or overdue**. Elsewhere, report and suggest nothing.

### What the analysis must show

This is the more valuable half. Per series:

```
Helix                                          31 products
  Revisions observed     2  (05 Mar 2026, 01 Sep 2026)
  Cadence                ~6 months
  Typical rate           7.94%   consistent (7.65% - 8.20%)
  Last revised           01 Sep 2026, 0 months ago
  Status                 NOT DUE - next expected around Mar 2027

SET                                           124 products
  Revisions observed     2
  Typical rate           41.4%   INCONSISTENT (6.6% - 59.7%)
  Status                 PATTERN NOT USABLE - this was a reset,
                         not a routine revision. No suggestion made.
```

**Say plainly where the pattern does not hold.** A series with an inconsistent history should produce no suggestion and an explanation, not an average of unlike things.

### Confidence, stated per series

```
STRONG     3+ revisions, spread under 1.5 points, consistent cadence
MODERATE   2 revisions, spread under 1.5 points
WEAK       2 revisions, spread 1.5-4 points
NONE       spread over 4 points, or fewer than 5 products, or one revision
```

Most series will be **MODERATE** today — two revisions is what the history holds. Say so rather than implying more.

## 3. Where the two models disagree — the real output

The historical model and the competitive model answer different questions. **Where they disagree is where a human should look.**

```
code   current   historical   competitive   note
151     336        363         314          historical +8%, competition says cut
1218D   2,490      2,690       2,910        competition has more room than our pattern
```

| Pattern | Means |
|---|---|
| Historical **above** competitive | Our usual increase would put us above the market |
| Historical **below** competitive | Competition has moved more than we have — headroom |
| The two agree | Strong signal; low-risk change |
| Historical only | No competitor mapped — pattern is the only guide |
| Competitive only | No usable series history |

Report the count in each, and list the largest disagreements by value.

**This is why the historical model is worth building.** On its own it just continues what Prayag already does. Against the competitive model it becomes a cross-check: two independent methods agreeing is worth more than either alone.

## 4. Comparison tab

A tab alongside the scenario list. Select **two or more** runs and compare.

```
                          Default    Sparsh only   Historical
products evaluated           819          712          1,904
would change                 694          503            887
median change             +6.2%        +5.8%          +8.0%
total value movement     +Rs 4.8 Cr   +Rs 3.1 Cr    +Rs 9.2 Cr

products where they differ                          412
  historical higher                                  287
  competitive higher                                 125
```

**Per product, on expansion:** what each run suggested and why — the rule or series that decided it.

Filters that matter: show only where they disagree, only where one has a suggestion and another does not, only products above a value threshold.

**Comparison must be read-only.** No merging, no "apply the best of each." A combined output nobody can trace to one policy is exactly what the immutable-run design exists to prevent.

---

## 5. What not to build

**No per-product trend fitting.** Two points is not a trend. Series level only.

**No extrapolation beyond one revision.** Suggesting what a price should be in three revisions' time compounds an estimate on an estimate.

**No blending the two models into one number.** Report both and their difference. A weighted average of a historical rate and a competitive target is defensible to nobody.

**No auto-apply.** Unchanged, and it stays unchanged.

---

## 6. Acceptance tests

```
1.  Compute MRP (Historical) produces a run with per-series analysis.
2.  A uniform series (Helix, 7.94%, spread 7.65-8.20) yields suggestions at
    its own rate, marked MODERATE.
3.  An inconsistent series (SET, spread 6.6-59.7) yields NO suggestion, with
    the reason stated.
4.  A series revised 0 months ago is marked NOT DUE, with the next expected
    date.
5.  No product-level trend is fitted; the series rate is applied.
6.  The comparison tab accepts two or more runs and reports where they differ.
7.  Products where historical and competitive disagree are listed, with both
    figures and both reasons.
8.  Comparison offers no merge or combined-apply action.
9.  Confidence is stated per series and reflects the number of revisions.
10. No price is written by either feature.
```

Test 3 is the one that keeps the model honest. A model that produces a number for every series, including the ones it cannot explain, is worse than one that admits where it does not know.
