## Two questions, and the second explains the first

**870 sold codes have no MRP, worth ₹11.03 Cr** — 328 of them selling steadily at ₹5.19 Cr. A product being sold should not be unpriceable.

**And the cadence question:** how often does this app fetch MRP from the pricing app, and what happens to a code the pricing app does not yet know about?

**One interpretation to apply throughout.** A code with no price effective in a period was **not offered** in that period. It is not a price of zero, and it is not missing data. A product with no price in March was not for sale in March.

## Section C — historic prices and the not-offered rule

```
RULE: a code with no price effective in a period was NOT OFFERED in that
period. Not zero, not missing.

C1. Does mrp_history support period-effective lookup - "what was this
    code's price in March 2026" - or does it hold only current prices with
    an effective date?

    The cache holds 6,109 rows from the pricing app. mrp_history was found
    stale and affecting only price-shrinker detection.

C2. FOR EACH OF THE 870, was there EVER a price?
      never priced in any period      -> new, or never offered
      priced previously, not now      -> withdrawn
      priced now, not when it sold    -> THE INTERESTING CASE

    The third group matters most: a product that sold in a month where it
    had no effective price.

C3. HOW DOES THE APP TREAT a sale in a period with no effective price?
    Nearest price, current price, or exclude the row?

    Using the CURRENT price against a past sale computes a discount against
    a price that did not exist at the time. Print which it does.

C4. Under the not-offered rule, those rows should be EXCLUDED from discount
    and realisation calculations, with the exclusion counted and stated -
    never silently priced at the nearest available figure.

    Print how much value would be excluded on that basis, per FY.
```

## What to read first, and why

**A2.** If the refresh is still restart-driven, the price catalogue is as fresh as the last deployment rather than the last price change — and that alone could explain codes the pricing app has and this one does not.

**B1.** Present in the pricing app but absent here is a defect on this side. Absent in both is a question for Prayag. One query separates them.

**C3 is the quiet risk.** Applying today's price to a past sale computes a discount against a price that did not exist then. The not-offered rule is the correct treatment, and it needs stating rather than assuming.

**B4 changes the headline.** Roughly ₹2.9 Cr of the ₹11.03 Cr is already accounted for. Quoting 870 unexplained codes overstates the gap; the residue is what to chase.
