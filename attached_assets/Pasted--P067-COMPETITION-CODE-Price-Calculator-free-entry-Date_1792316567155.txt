# P067 · COMPETITION · CODE — Price Calculator free entry

**Date:** 18 October 2026
**Scope:** `artifacts/prayag-price-finder` Price Calculator page **only**, plus whatever server
endpoints that page alone calls.
**Extends:** P058 (calculator) and the unit-badge revisions of 16 October.
**Depends on:** nothing. Can land before or after P066, but see §10.

---

## 0. Why, and the one rule that does not bend

A sales person is standing in a retailer's shop. The retailer names competitors the app has never
heard of and quotes prices the app does not hold, and wants a comparison **now**. The calculator has
to be able to produce one. So: free-typed competitors, free-typed item codes, and every MRP editable.

That means the calculator will contain two kinds of number that look identical on screen:

- **verified** — read from the catalogue or the competitor tables, with provenance behind it;
- **asserted** — typed by a sales person, often repeating what an interested party in the room told
  them.

### The rule

> **Nothing typed into the calculator is ever written to `catalog_products`,
> `competitor_prices`, `mrp_price_history`, the competitor master, or any coverage/analytics
> aggregate. Not on save, not on export, not on "confirm". Free entry is basket-local, always.**

Everything else in this prompt is detail. This is the part that must not be traded away for
convenience, and if any instruction below seems to require breaking it, stop and report instead.

The second rule follows from the first: **asserted numbers must be visibly distinguishable from
verified ones, on screen and in every export.** An export that looks like a company price comparison
but contains hand-typed competitor prices will be shown to retailers as if it were company data.
That is the failure mode to design against.

---

## 1. What must not change

- The Price Finder page, its bucket/company enforcement, and its competitor scoping — **untouched.**
- Coverage statistics, competitor price history, analytics, any dashboard — **untouched** and must
  not see calculator free entry.
- The exact item-code matching rule from P062 §3 still applies here in full: **no normalisation, no
  punctuation-insensitive or case-insensitive matching.** See §4.
- Unit-first rounding from P058: `netUnit = round(mrp × (1 − disc/100), 2)`, then
  `rowTotal = round(netUnit × qty, 2)`. Unchanged for asserted prices too.
- The denominator discipline from P058 §7.2 — a competitor's percentage is computed against the
  subtotal of the products that competitor was actually compared on, never the whole basket.

---

## 2. Editable MRP

Every MRP field in the calculator becomes editable — ours and every competitor's.

- **Default is the app value**, loaded as today. The field is pre-filled, not blank.
- Editing it changes the basket row only.
- **An edited field must show it has been edited** and must offer a one-click revert to the app
  value. Show the app value alongside, e.g. `28.00 (app: 27.80)` — the sales person needs to see what
  they overrode.
- An MRP with no app value behind it (ad-hoc product, ad-hoc competitor) is blank and required
  before the row can be included in a total.
- Validate: positive number, two decimals, reject zero and negative. Do not cap the upper bound —
  some SKUs are genuinely in the thousands.

### Three states, one vocabulary

Use these three states everywhere — screen, summary, export. Do not invent per-surface wording.

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **App** | app value, unedited |
| **Edited** | app value existed, user changed it |
| **Entered** | no app value; user supplied it |

## 3. Ad-hoc competitors

Allow adding a competitor by typing a name that is not in the database.

- The competitor picker gains a free-text path: if the typed name matches no known competitor, offer
  **"Add '<name>' as a one-off competitor"**.
- **If the typed name does match a known competitor, offer the known one first and prominently.**
  A sales person typing "Astral" must not end up with an ad-hoc Astral row sitting next to real
  Astral data. They may still choose ad-hoc deliberately — allow it, but make the known option the
  obvious default.
- An ad-hoc competitor requires a typed MRP. Discount and qty behave exactly as now.
- **Do not create a competitor record.** No row in the competitor master, no brand id, no
  auto-registration on save. The name is free text on the basket row, flagged ad-hoc.
- Ad-hoc competitor names are **not** matched, merged or deduplicated against real brands anywhere.
  Two sales people typing "Jindal Pipes" and "jindal" produce two unrelated ad-hoc entries, and that
  is correct — the alternative is guessing.

### Unit and basis for ad-hoc competitors

An ad-hoc competitor has no recorded unit and no recorded basis. So:

- the unit badge is the **neutral "unit not recorded"** state, never amber — there is nothing to
  disagree with;
- but add a distinct note on the row: **"price entered by user — basis and unit unverified"**. This
  is a stronger statement than the existing neutral note and must read differently from it.

The amber "units differ" badge stays reserved for what it was built for: two recorded units that
disagree, as with the 323 Astral pipe-length rows.

---

## 4. Ad-hoc products / item codes

Allow adding a product whose item code is not in the catalogue, or is in the catalogue but has no
price.

Two different cases, handled differently:

**4a. Code exists in the catalogue, no price held.** Resolve the product normally — item code,
description, division, category all come from the catalogue. Only the MRP is **Entered**. This is
the common case and it keeps full product provenance.

**4b. Code matches nothing.** The user supplies item code (free text), a description, and an MRP.
The row is a fully ad-hoc product.

- It has **no division and no category.** Do not guess them, do not infer them from the code shape,
  do not assign a default. Group these under **"Ad-hoc"** in any grouped display.
- **Match the typed code exactly, or not at all.** If the typed code exactly equals a catalogue
  `item_code`, resolve to that product (case 4a). Otherwise it is ad-hoc. Never fuzzy-match,
  never strip whitespace or punctuation to find a candidate. `TTS - 01` typed is `TTS - 01`, not
  `TTS-01`. You may *suggest* near matches in the picker as a ranking hint, but a suggestion the user
  does not accept must never silently resolve the row.

## 5. Bucket scope — removed here, and disclosed

Correct: bucket/company enforcement does not apply in the calculator. A sales person comparing
against whatever the retailer names cannot be held to a saved bucket list.

So: **the calculator searches the full catalogue and the full competitor set, with no bucket,
company-enforcement or saved-selection filtering.**

**But the disclosure line must change, not disappear.** P058 shipped a disclosure stating the
calculator was bucket-scoped. Leaving that line in place while removing the scope is worse than
either choice on its own. Replace it with something to this effect:

> This calculator searches the full product catalogue and all competitors, without bucket or company
> filters, and allows one-off competitors and products typed in by hand. It is a quoting aid, not a
> source of record.

Keep the other four P058 disclosure lines as they are.

## 6. Provenance on screen and in export

### Screen

- Each price cell carries its state from §2 — **App / Edited / Entered**.
- Ad-hoc competitor and ad-hoc product rows are marked as such, visibly, not only on hover.
- **If the basket contains any Edited or Entered value, show a banner above the summary**, not
  buried at the bottom: *"This comparison includes N hand-entered or edited prices."*

### Excel export

- A **provenance column per price**: `App` / `Edited` / `Entered`. Not a footnote — a column.
- For Edited: a second column holding the app value that was overridden, so the delta is recoverable.
- Ad-hoc competitors and ad-hoc products flagged in their own columns.
- **A banner row at the top of both sheets** when any Edited or Entered value is present, stating
  the count and that those figures are unverified. Above the data, visible without scrolling.
- The existing neutral/amber unit notes stay, with the §3 ad-hoc note as a third distinct value.

---

## 7. What the Final Summary must say

Keep everything P058 §7 specifies, and add:

- counts: how many prices are **App**, **Edited**, **Entered**;
- how many competitors in the basket are **ad-hoc**, by name;
- how many products are **ad-hoc**;
- and — this one matters — the existing "compared on N of M products" line must **count ad-hoc
  products in M and say how many of the M are ad-hoc.** "Compared on 3 of 5 products, 2 of which
  were hand-entered" is honest. "Compared on 3 of 5" is not, when two were typed in the shop.

Percentage differences are still computed against the subtotal of the compared products only. That
does not change because some of them are asserted.

---

## 8. Hard prohibitions

Do not, under any framing:

- write any calculator value to `catalog_products`, `competitor_prices`, `mrp_price_history`, the
  competitor master, or any aggregate;
- create a competitor record, brand id, or product record from calculator input;
- add a "save this price to the database" or "capture this competitor" action — **not even behind a
  permission check.** That is a separate piece of work with its own review path (§9);
- feed calculator free entry into coverage counts, dashboards or the Price Finder;
- normalise, trim or case-fold an item code to resolve it;
- infer division or category for an ad-hoc product;
- apply bucket filtering inside the calculator, or leave the old bucket disclosure line in place.

---

## 9. Logged, explicitly not now

Hand-typed competitor prices from the field are genuinely valuable market intelligence, and it is
tempting to capture them. **Do not.** Promoting asserted prices into the competitor tables needs a
review queue, an attribution trail, and a decision about whether a retailer's word is an acceptable
source — three questions this prompt does not answer. Note it and move on.

Also still logged from earlier, unchanged: `unit` → `NULL` at `apolloStagedLoad.ts` and
`catalogImportExport.ts` plus the 1,360-row backfill; the 38 development fixtures; relaxing the
zero-composite block to a warning; the 20 split-spelling category groups and 394 blank categories.

## 11. Tests

Add to the existing calculator suite:

1. Edited MRP: arithmetic uses the edited value, unit-first rounding, revert restores the app value.
2. Entered MRP on a priceless catalogue product (4a): division and category still come from the
   catalogue.
3. Ad-hoc product (4b): no division, no category, groups under Ad-hoc, exact-match rule holds —
   typing `TTS - 01` does not resolve to `TTS-01`.
4. Ad-hoc competitor: no competitor record created; neutral unit badge plus the ad-hoc note; never
   amber.
5. Typing a name matching a known competitor offers the known one first.
6. Export: provenance column present and correct for all three states; overridden app value
   retained; banner row present when any Edited/Entered exists and absent when none.
7. Summary: App/Edited/Entered counts correct; "N of M" counts ad-hoc products in M and discloses
   how many were hand-entered.
8. **A persistence test asserting that a full calculator session with ad-hoc competitors, ad-hoc
   products and edited MRPs leaves `catalog_products`, `competitor_prices`, `mrp_price_history` and
   the competitor master byte-identical.** This is the test that protects §0.
