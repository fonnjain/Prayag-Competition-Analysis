# P015 — COMPETITION — Matching MRP workbench, category scope, and margin in comparison

> **P015** · app: **COMPETITION** (`prayag-price.com`)
> **THIS PROMPT CHANGES CODE.**
> Quote `P015` when reporting back.

Three additions. A **Matching MRP** view for working through a category by hand. **Category scoping** everywhere. And **collective margin** in the comparison, so scenarios can be judged on money rather than on counts.

---

## 1. Matching MRP — a workbench, not a rule engine

The scenario builder answers *"apply this policy across the catalogue."* This answers a different question: **"show me this category against these competitors, and let me decide product by product."**

Both are needed. A pricing manager working through CP Fittings does not want to write a rule; they want to see the list and make calls.

### The flow

```
1. Pick a category          the same browse control as Price Finder
                            CP Fittings / Faucets 1040 · Pipes & Fittings 2278 ...

2. Pick competitors         one or several. Sparsh Pearl, Ashirvad, or both

3. See where we stand       every product in the category, current MRP
                            against each selected competitor

4. Decide, per product      match · less by N% · leave · set a price
```

### The table

```
CODE     PRODUCT              PRAYAG   SPARSH   ASHIRVAD   POSITION    ACTION
1218D    Sink Cock 15mm        2,490    2,310      2,180    +14.2% ▲   [ match ▾ ]
151      Ball Cock 15mm          336      330          -     +1.8% ▲   [ -3%   ▾ ]
124-V    Bib Cock Long           422      468          -    -9.8% ▼    [ leave ▾ ]
```

**Default the sort to where Prayag is most above the competition.** That is what the view is for, and it should open on the answer rather than on an alphabetical list.

**Show both directions.** Where Prayag is *below* the competition is where the money is — 130 of 150 against Sparsh, 161 of 171 on Ashirvad CPVC. Provide a filter: *above only* · *below only* · *all*. Default to *above only*, since that is the stated need, but do not hide the rest.

### Per-product action

```
match                 set to the selected competitor's price
less by N%            competitor price minus N
leave                 no change, recorded as reviewed
set                   type a price directly
```

With several competitors selected, the user chooses which drives the action — lowest, highest, or a named one — per product or for the whole view.

**Bulk actions on the current filter**, because working 1,040 products one at a time is not realistic: *apply "3% less than Sparsh" to everything currently shown*, then adjust individuals.

### It produces a scenario

The result saves as a scenario like any other — immutable, comparable, and handed off through the existing revision preview. **It is a different way to build a scenario, not a different way to write prices.**

Record per product that the action was manual, and by whom. A hand-set price and a rule-derived price should not look alike six weeks later.

## 2. Category scope everywhere

Add the same category selector to the scenario builder and to Compute MRP (Historical).

Today scope offers division, series and explicit codes. Category is the level people actually think in — *"CP Fittings"*, not *"CP Fittings / Faucets | PTMT & Plastic Fittings"*.

Note the catalogue has **compound divisions** — `Ceramic Sanitaryware | PTMT & Plastic Fittings` with 50 products, and three more. Decide whether a product in a compound division appears under each of its parts or only under the compound, and say which. Either is defensible; silently picking one is not.

## 4. Collective margin — yes, with two assumptions on the face of it

This is possible, and it is the figure that makes comparison meaningful. Counts do not tell you which scenario is better; money does.

### The calculation

```
unit_cost      = bom_value / qty                    from the margin feed
current        = (avg_sale − unit_cost) / avg_sale

new_avg_sale   = avg_sale × (suggested_mrp / current_mrp)
projected      = (new_avg_sale − unit_cost) / new_avg_sale

collective     = Σ(qty × (price − unit_cost)) / Σ(qty × price)
                 weighted by sales quantity from the chosen period
```

### The two assumptions, stated on screen

**Discount holds.** An 8% MRP increase is assumed to lift the realised price 8%. That holds if dealer discounts are a constant percentage. If they are absolute amounts, or get renegotiated when MRP moves, it does not.

**Volume holds.** No elasticity data exists, so the projection assumes quantities are unchanged. Raising prices 8% may well reduce volume; nothing here can say by how much.

**Both must appear beside the number, not in a footnote.** A projected margin is a conditional statement, and presenting it as a forecast would be the single most misleading thing this application could do.

### Coverage must travel with it

```
margin feed   3,159 codes, Apr-Jun 2026, PTMT Apr held
sales feed    3,138 codes, Apr-Aug 2026
catalogue     6,106 codes
```

Computable only on products that have **margin, sales, and a suggestion**. Report the count and, more importantly, **the share of scope value it covers**:

```
Collective margin    44.1% → 45.3%    +1.2 points
                     computed on 1,847 products = 61% of scope value
                     assumes discount % and volume unchanged
```

A +1.2 point improvement on 61% of value is a different claim from one on 95%, and the reader must be able to tell them apart.

### In the comparison table

```
                         Default   Sparsh only   Historical   CP matching
products changing            694          503          887           212
median change             +6.2%        +5.8%        +8.0%         -2.1%
collective margin         +1.2pt       +0.9pt       +1.8pt        -0.4pt
  coverage                   61%          58%          64%           71%
value at risk           Rs 2.1 Cr    Rs 1.4 Cr    Rs 3.8 Cr    Rs 0.6 Cr
```

**Value at risk** — the sales value of products whose price rises, which is where volume loss would bite if the volume assumption fails. Not a prediction, an exposure.

## 5. What not to build

**No elasticity estimate.** Fitting demand to observational sales data would encode past pricing and look rigorous while being circular.

**No single "best scenario" score.** Ranking scenarios by projected margin invites picking the number without reading the assumptions under it.

**No merging.** Comparison stays read-only. A combined output traceable to no single policy is what the immutable-run design exists to prevent.

**No auto-apply.** Unchanged.

## 6. Acceptance tests

```
1.  Matching MRP opens on a category and lists every product against the
    selected competitors.
2.  Default sort is most-above-competition first; the filter offers above,
    below and all.
3.  Per-product actions work: match, less by N%, leave, set.
4.  A bulk action applies to the current filter only, and individuals can be
    changed afterwards.
5.  A Matching MRP session saves as a comparable scenario, with manual
    actions recorded as manual and attributed.
6.  Category scope appears in the scenario builder and in Historical, and
    compound divisions behave as documented.
7.  Comparison accepts a mixture of rule, historical and matching runs.
8.  Collective margin is reported with its coverage share and both
    assumptions visible.
9.  A product without margin or sales data is excluded from the margin
    figure and counted, not assumed.
10. Value at risk is reported per scenario.
11. No merge, no ranking, no auto-apply.
12. No price is written by any of it.
```

Test 8 is the one that keeps this defensible. Test 12 has not changed.
