## 2. Run the default and report the direction

The default scenario reports **819 products in scope, 694 would change** — 85%. That is high enough to need explaining before anyone acts on it.

Run it. Report:

```
Of the 694 changing:
  increases       count, median %, total value movement
  decreases       count, median %, total value movement
  by competitor   Sparsh-matched vs Ashirvad-matched
  by division
```

**What the answer means.**

If most are **increases**, the default is capturing margin we are leaving on the table — consistent with Prayag being cheaper on 130 of 150 against Sparsh and 161 of 171 on Ashirvad CPVC. That is the expected result.

If most are **decreases**, the default rule is cutting prices on products already below the competition, which nobody intends. Say so plainly if that is what you find.

Also report the **125 not changing** — held by a limit, at parity, or no competitor price.

## 3. Isolate Ashirvad

Ashirvad's list is **563 days old** and its CPVC gaps run +43% to +50%. Those may be a real market position or a stale price list.

Run two more scenarios, identical to the default but for the competitor:

```
B — Sparsh Pearl only
C — Ashirvad only
```

Report for each: products in scope, how many change, increases and decreases, median movement.

**Then the question that matters:** how many of the default's 694 changes depend on Ashirvad being the nearest competitor? Those are the suggestions resting entirely on an eighteen-month-old list.

Use the comparison feature — this is what it is for.

## 4. Turn margin floors on

Re-run the default with **margin floors ticked**, everything else unchanged.

```
                        margin off    margin on
products changing            694          ?
escalations (floor blocks
   the competitive target)     -          ?
excluded, no margin data       0          ?
```

**The escalations are the valuable output.** A product whose competitive target would breach its category floor needs a decision, not a price. Report them individually — code, current MRP, competitive target, the floor that blocked it, and the gap between the two.

Also report **portfolio margin before and after**, with the share of scope value it was computed on. Margin covers Apr–Jun 2026 with PTMT Apr held, so that share will not be 100% and the figure means little without it.

## 5. Sanity-check ten products by hand

Pick ten changing products spanning both competitors and at least three divisions. For each, show the full chain:

```
code · current MRP and its effective date
competitor used, its price, its date, its age
the gap
the rule row that matched
the suggested price and the arithmetic
any limit or floor that bound it
```

**This is the check that matters most.** Run totals can look reasonable while individual suggestions are wrong. If the arithmetic does not survive reading ten products end to end, the totals are not trustworthy either.

Include at least one where a limit bound the result, and one escalation if section 4 produced any.

## 6. What not to do

**Do not send anything to the revision flow.** These runs are for understanding, not for applying.

**Do not tune the default to make the numbers look better.** If 694 of 819 is wrong, the finding is *why*, not a quieter default.

**Do not enable Ashirvad-only capture.** It stays off until a current list arrives.
